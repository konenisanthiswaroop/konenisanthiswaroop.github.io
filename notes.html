3GPP Explained: RedCap, eRedCap and eDRX

Plain-language notes on how 5G NR serves devices that do not need full 5G speed. Written from public 3GPP information. Figures are typical values; always check the specification for exact numbers.

Why these features exist

Full 5G NR devices are built for very high speeds, so they need wide bandwidth, several antennas and a lot of power. Many devices do not need that: smartwatches, industrial sensors, video cameras and wearables. These features give the network a cheaper, simpler, longer-lasting device class that still lives on the same 5G network.

RedCap (Reduced Capability, Release 17)

Often called "NR-Light". It is a simpler 5G device type that sits between full 5G and low-power IoT (LTE-M / NB-IoT).

  • Smaller bandwidth: up to 20 MHz in FR1 (100 MHz in FR2) instead of 100 MHz or more.
  • Fewer antennas: 1 or 2 receive branches and 1 transmit, so fewer MIMO layers.
  • Optional relaxed features such as half-duplex FDD and lower modulation in some cases.
  • Network side: the cell must tell RedCap devices they are allowed, and the network needs to recognise them early (during random access) so it can schedule them within their limits.

eRedCap (enhanced RedCap, Release 18)

Takes the idea further for even simpler, lower-cost, lower-power devices.

  • Much smaller bandwidth: 5 MHz in FR1.
  • Lower peak data rate, on the order of 10 Mbps, with a single receive branch and a single layer.
  • Targets wearables and low-end industrial devices, closing the gap towards LTE-M class devices.
  • Network side: it must recognise the new device type, broadcast its own access rules, and keep scheduling within the small bandwidth.

eDRX (extended Discontinuous Reception)

DRX lets a device sleep and wake periodically to check for paging. Extended DRX stretches the sleep much longer to save battery.

  • In idle mode the cycle can reach about 2.9 hours.
  • In RRC_INACTIVE the cycle is up to about 10.24 seconds.
  • Trade-off: longer sleep saves power but delays how quickly the network can reach the device.
  • The network and device must agree on the cycle and paging timing so pages are not missed.
